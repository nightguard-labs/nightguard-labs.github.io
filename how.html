How I figure things out

I started with no background in programming, AI or neuroscience. I work things out one step at a time: if this is true, then this has to come next. This page keeps track of how the design came together and the calls that shaped it.

The method

Start from the limit, then follow the logic

I start from the constraint I can't change and ask what has to be true to get around it. Then I think in network layers: each layer gets one job, every part speaks the same protocol, traffic gets its own lanes so nothing blocks, and anything a layer can't handle moves up the stack.

Nov 2026

The starting point: 32 GB I can't upgrade

My MacBook has 32 GB of RAM and that's final. I saw that other people and companies had designed ways around limits like that, so I asked the obvious question.

What's stopping me from making my own way?

Every piece of the project exists because of that one limit. With unlimited RAM there'd be no need for streaming, relays or a stack.

Nov 2026

Network layering became the brain's layout

I didn't copy this from anywhere. Each idea from network layering had a direct counterpart:

Layers with one job, escalating what they can't handle→The brain stack: 9B → 35B → 80B → 122B One standard protocol between all parts→Everything native Swift + Metal Separate links so traffic never blocks→8 SSD lanes, plus a separate relay for rarely used experts Caching close to where it's used→Hot experts in RAM, the rest on the SSD Failover→A task moves up a layer when its checks fail
Every part needs to be the same for the best communication.
Nov 2026

It turned out to look like a brain

Only after the design came together did I find out it mirrors how the human brain is organized: fast reflexes handle the easy stuff, slow deliberate thinking handles the hard stuff, memories get consolidated during sleep, and old memories take longer to recall. I got there from the logic alone, which tells me it's the natural answer to the problem: handle the common things fast and cheap, and only escalate what's hard.

Nov 2026

Calls that paid off

Converting the 9B to Swift. I asked whether moving the small model into the same Swift engine would help. Measured result: 2.1× faster (32.5 vs 15.5 tok/s). Its quality at 4-bit is still being tested.

Not wasting test time. I cut a re-test of the 122B from the final round because it couldn't change any decision. That freed hours of compute for the tests that mattered.

Making speed part of "done." Working wasn't enough for me. I added a hard requirement that small tasks finish in under 10 minutes.

At the end it needs to be usable, not waiting 40 minutes for it to get something done.

Putting the 80B before the 122B. The 80B scored the same as the 122B on the coding check at about 3× the speed. So it goes first, and the slow giant becomes the last resort.

Cleaning up as I go. Once a model is converted and verified, the original download gets deleted so the disk doesn't fill up. That freed 45 GB the same night.
